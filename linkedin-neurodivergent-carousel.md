"Your current training is built for one type of brain."

**Body:**

- High-performing people fail because the *format* is wrong

- Compliance training becomes a barrier instead of a tool

---

## Slide 3: The Five Barriers
**Headline:**
"The five learning barriers we remove."

**List (with icons):**
1. **ATTENTION** — Can't stay focused through long modules
2. **COMMUNICATION** — Jargon and complex language shut people down
3. **EXECUTIVE FUNCTION** — No visible roadmap = no follow-through
4. **ACCESS** — No captions, no downloadable resources, no mobile option
5. **ASSESSMENT** — Written test = fail, even if they know the material

---

## Slide 4: The Solution
**Headline:**
"PulseOS Learning Intelligence redesigns courses around how people actually learn."

**Body:**
Instead of hoping your course works for everyone:

→ Capture how your learners focus
→ Understand their communication needs
→ Map their executive function supports
→ Build in multiple access options
→ Offer assessment choices

Result: Training that works for *all* brains.

---

## Slide 5: Design in Action
**Headline:**
"What inclusive learning actually looks like."

**Before / After Grid:**

| Barrier | Before | After |
|---------|--------|-------|
| **Attention** | 45-min dense module | 8-10 min focused units with breaks |
| **Communication** | Technical jargon | Plain language + visuals + examples |
| **Executive Function** | Vague deadlines | Checklists, milestones, visible progress |
| **Access** | Desktop only | Mobile + captions + downloadable PDFs |
| **Assessment** | One-shot written test | Choice: write, perform, discuss |

---

## Slide 6: Who Benefits
**Headline:**
"This isn't just for neurodivergent learners."

**Body:**
Inclusive design helps *everyone*:
- New parents managing time → executive function supports
- Second-language learners → visual + plain language
- Older workers returning to workforce → accessible digital options
- Anxious learners → predictable structure
- High-performing people → choice in how they demonstrate knowledge

Neurodivergent-friendly is *human*-friendly.

## Design Notes for Each Slide

**Slide 1 (Hook):**
- Navy background, gold accent
- Large bold headline
- Keep body minimal
- Brain icon + accessibility icon
- Emphasis on "It never has"

**Slide 2 (Problem):**
- Light background (gold-50)
- Red/warning accent for pain points
- Use a stat callout box
- Simple bullet points

**Slide 3 (Barriers):**
- 5-column layout with icons
- Each barrier gets an icon (brain, chat, checklist, mobile, check)
- Short, punchy labels
- Light gray background

**Slide 4 (Solution):**
- Navy background, gold accents
- Use arrows (→) to show flow
- Step-by-step progression
- Highlight "all brains" phrase

**Slide 5 (Design in Action):**
- Clean before/after table
- Left column = problem, right column = solution
- Use gold for "After" column background
- Small icons for each row

**Slide 6 (Who Benefits):**
- White/soft gray background
- Bullet points with personas
- Emphasize final line: "human-friendly"
- Include diverse imagery if possible (different age groups, roles)

**Slide 7 (CTA):**
- Navy background, gold CTA button
- Include PulseOS logo
- Direct link
- Keep hashtags relevant to LinkedIn algorithm (10 max, but 5-7 better)
